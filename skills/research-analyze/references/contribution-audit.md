# Contribution Audit and Research Iteration

Read this for strengthening an existing paper, explaining mixed or negative
results, assessing theoretical value, or choosing further research. Use the
[shared round contract](../../research-navigate/references/workflow-contract.md)
and [stage routes](../../research-navigate/references/stage-map.md).

## Inventory the project's evidence

Start with all known project-related repositories, configured server directories,
manuscript versions, data, code, run manifests, figures, appendices and failed
attempts. Identify missing roots from project records; do not expand into
unrelated server projects. Use actual authorized access and available tools.

For each asset or coherent batch, record its identity/location, version, related
claims, coverage (discovered, inspected, partially inspected or inaccessible),
what was inspected and what remains. A listing or manifest establishes discovery,
not verified contents. Attribute user-reported inspection to its source rather
than claiming to have performed it.

Process large datasets in bounded, resumable batches with stable identities.
Reconcile coverage against the inventory, including missing and corrupt assets;
distinguish sampled inspection from exhaustive checks. Trace headline values to
raw records and transformations, including exclusion reasons and failed attempts.
Keep useful local analysis moving when a remote dependency is unavailable.

## Audit the whole argument

Include the [focus anchor](../../research-focus/references/thesis-anchor.md)
and cumulative thesis changes in the author-side audit. Record which important
theme question each core contribution answers. A tool or calibration result may
support that question without replacing it. Reframing a null result requires the
same evidential care as a positive claim; a plausible mechanism stays a hypothesis.

Read the available abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, figures,
appendices and research assets. Audit substantive claims and content units;
necessary background need not make a novel contribution. Mark absent sections.

Produce a compact contribution table with:

- Location and current claim or content.
- Role: core contribution, supporting evidence, necessary context,
  redundant/off-topic material, or unverified claim.
- Contribution type when relevant: method, explanation/mechanism, boundary
  conditions, resource trade-off, evaluation insight, theory or reusable asset.
- Linked evidence and separate status: supported, limited, contradicted or
  unresolved; include counterevidence, uncertainty and scope.
- Action: retain, strengthen, combine, move to appendix or remove, with a reason.

Identify actual strengths before deciding how to improve the contribution.
Calibration or reproducibility can be valuable without automatically being novel.
Use inspected closest work to assess distinctiveness; missing novelty evidence
routes to research-survey rather than an assertion of originality.

## Turn observations into defensible explanations

Organize proposed framing as **observation -> explanation -> conditions ->
meaning**. State the observed effect and uncertainty first. Separate a supported
explanation from competing hypotheses and the evidence that could distinguish
them. Retain unfavorable outcomes and conditions where the method does not help.
Changing tone does not create evidence.

Example: equal observed averages may coexist with opposing task-level effects.
If grouping was selected after seeing results, it is an exploratory clue about
conditions, not an established interaction or mechanism. Preserve the grouping
history and alternatives such as selection noise. Independent confirmation may
support a conditional effect; causal attribution or a theorem has its own evidence
or proof obligations. A null estimate alone establishes neither equivalence nor
an explanation of failure.

Offer candidate wording and its evidence links here. Hand substantial manuscript
edits to research-write; do not promote unapproved principal conclusions through
a prose change.

## Compare improvement opportunities

Compare relevant existing-data analysis, mechanism/ablation/boundary tests,
fairer controls, theory, evaluation work and reusable assets. Rank by the
importance of the scientific question, expected information gain, evidence gap,
feasibility and resource cost, not by how positive the expected result sounds.
Separate mandatory validity repairs, valuable enhancements and low-value extras.

For each recommended opportunity state the targeted claim, expected learning,
supporting/contradicting evidence, minimal discriminating check, required inputs,
cost estimate or explicit unknown, authorization and priority. Explain which
judgment about the paper changes if it succeeds. Prefer a cheap decisive check
before a large expansion; deciding against extra work is a valid recommendation.

For theoretical work, additionally state the phenomenon to explain, distinction
from inspected prior work, assumptions and their relevance, exact proof obligation
or testable prediction, and what a counterexample would mean. Distinguish a proved
result, conjecture and empirical association. A proof under assumptions unrelated
to the observations may add little value. Do not require theory for every paper
or run empirical experiments for a claim whose missing evidence is a proof.

Evaluate acceptance/score requests through contribution importance, novelty,
validity, clarity, fair comparison and reproducibility. If a venue is specified,
verify its actual applicable criteria and record the source/version; when these
are unavailable, state that limit and use general quality dimensions. Without
reliable calibration, provide qualitative risks and priorities rather than
numerical acceptance probabilities or predicted scores. Keep all relevant
attempts; outcome selection cannot be a contribution-improvement tactic.

## Deliver the next round and advance it

Link four outputs rather than duplicating project records: evidence coverage,
contribution table, ranked opportunities, and the shared round package. Include
support/refutation/inconclusive branches, cost/remaining allowance and completion
or stopping conditions for the chosen next action.

Each outcome branch specifies an action, not only an interpretation: continue,
close or pivot, with the learning that justifies any further work. For an
inconclusive result, identify an affordable discriminating next check or close
with the remaining uncertainty; uncertainty alone is not a reason to extend a
study. A refutation may retire the tested explanation while preserving a useful
negative result. Proposed scope or resource changes still need their decision.

Continue authorized existing-data work in analyze. Coordinate missing source
checks, revised design/proofs, implementation or approved experiments through
research-navigate. When tools or inputs are absent, deliver the executable
package with an explicit unexecuted status and the missing prerequisite.

After each action, reconcile actual artifacts and consumption, compare the new
claim-evidence map with the previous revision, and decide whether another round
can change a meaningful judgment. Preserve exploratory analyses and protect
independent confirmation. Do not repeatedly search until a preferred result or
significance threshold appears.

Project-specific completion may be a reliable benefit, explanation, boundary,
credible negative result, proof or useful evaluation asset. Move supported and
approved findings to writing when the agreed evidence goal is met; consolidate
independent review at the applicable M3/M4/M5 milestone rather than after each
analysis action. Startup delegation can cover in-scope conclusion formation.
For a full-paper Goal, an improved contribution map is intermediate work; continue
to the actual paper, two venue-review rounds and reproduction package.
Recommend closure or a pivot when additional work lacks reasonable information
value. Apply the shared resource/validity stops to dependent execution while
continuing independent authorized work.
