# Analyze Evidence and Strengthen Contributions

Read the [shared contract](../research-navigate/references/workflow-contract.md)
and applicable [domain references](../research-navigate/references/domains/index.md).
Read [model routing](../research-navigate/references/model-routing.md) for native
analysis branches and independent review, and the
[reuse and simplification audit](../research-navigate/references/reuse-and-simplification.md)
when prior results may simplify current mathematics, code, experiments or claims.
At a genuine candidate choice - which interpretation, reuse decision or next round
to pursue - read
[evidence comparison and optional Jev](../research-navigate/references/decision-support.md).
Use inspected evidence and program constraints first, a bounded native comparison
when useful, then the integrator decision. Comparison has no proof or gate authority.

## Choose the requested depth

Keep a bounded calculation or interpretation focused on its supplied evidence.
For paper-wide contribution improvement, explanatory framing, theoretical value,
or further research planning, read the
[contribution audit](references/contribution-audit.md). Use that mode to examine
strengths and gaps across project evidence and produce an actionable next round.
Pure prose editing belongs to research-write; independent scientific criticism
belongs to research-review.

## Analyze the actual record

For a project's next-round or contribution audit, use
[research-focus](../research-focus/SKILL.md) to compare the current argument and
cumulative pivots with the stable theme. Distinguish an important narrower or
negative conclusion from an easier substitute problem. Preserve uncertainty:
neither on-theme relevance nor explanatory wording proves a mechanism.

1. Inspect the approved protocol, raw records, versions, selection rules and run
   manifest. Reconcile every attempt and explain missingness/exclusions before
   aggregation. Preserve original records.
2. Match comparison conditions, units, task/split/seed identities and resource
   budgets. Flag protocol or baseline mismatches before attributing differences
   to the proposed method.
3. Compute appropriate summaries and uncertainty for the sampling design. Distinguish
   nested observations from independent replicates and planned tests from
   post-outcome exploration. Do not impose a universal repetition count or test.
4. Use relevant ablations, sensitivity, failure and mechanism analyses to assess
   alternative explanations. Separate practical effect, statistical uncertainty,
   causal identification and scope of generalization.
5. Build a claim-evidence map: proposed statement, supporting observations and
   calculations, contradicting evidence, uncertainty, applicable conditions and
   status (supported, limited, contradicted or unresolved).
   Carry estimand, denominator, dependence structure, uncertainty type and level.
   Apply [the evidence ledger](../research-navigate/references/evidence-ledger.md)
   when support is disputed or incomplete; do not infer what an untyped `+/-` means.
6. Recommend continuation, targeted evidence, redesign, reframing or stopping.
   Negative results can complete an analysis; inconclusive evidence is not proof
   of equivalence or absence of an effect.
7. Compare current derivations, implementations, experiments and claims with
   reusable prior work. Report the actual simplification, the condition checks,
   what contribution remains, and which experiments still provide unique evidence.
   Do not delete adverse or negative evidence because a simpler narrative exists.

When results appear to contradict theory, check the exact definition/assumption
revision and the claim's quantifiers before interpreting them. Separate a proof
defect, a model assumption not met, an implementation/configuration discrepancy
and an in-domain counterexample; do not infer which occurred without evidence.
Empirical support and proof-check status are separate. Preserve adverse results
and identify affected proofs, tests and paper claims when assumptions change.
Route source/transfer gaps to survey, mathematical repair to design, code issues
to implement, and a changed research question to ideate.

## Deliver and continue

Deliver computed tables/figures with source and transformation references,
uncertainty/validity notes, the claim-evidence map and a decision recommendation.
Include the shared handoff and actual execution status of any analysis code.
Consolidate principal conclusions at M3 before adopting them as settled findings;
route findings covered by startup delegation or a specific decision to write,
and evidence gaps to the appropriate stage. Do not ask again for routine
in-scope conclusion formation already delegated by the user.
Use a reviewer independent of the analysis branch for the principal-conclusions
gate, not after every calculation or contribution hypothesis. Inspect the actual tables, records, derivations and
simplification evidence, not only the analysis summary.

In contribution mode, also deliver evidence coverage, the contribution audit,
ranked opportunities and the shared round package. Compare new understanding
with the previous round. Continue feasible work already covered by the current
request, protocol and resources; use research-navigate to coordinate dependent
stages. A proposal is not execution; unsettled findings remain provisional until
the applicable milestone and authority are satisfied. Respect an analysis-only
boundary. In a full-paper Goal, completing the audit is an intermediate outcome,
not the end of manuscript, two-round review or reproduction-package work.

Example: a headline averaged across tasks should preserve per-task effects and
resource conditions. If a gain comes only from one expensive task setting, state
that dependency instead of presenting it as uniform improvement.
