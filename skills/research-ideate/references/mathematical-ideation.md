# Mathematical Ideation and Theory Prototypes

Default to this approach for open-ended new research directions and methods.
The preference is rigorous definitions and a serious theory attempt before formal
experiments, with small calculations and counterexample searches allowed to refine
the model. It is not a requirement to finish every proof before empirical work.
Honor explicitly empirical, replication, reading-only and pure-theory requests.

## Formalize what the candidate actually asserts

For each candidate worth serious comparison, specify the relevant objects/spaces,
variables and domains, time/sample indices, probability structure if any,
operators/objectives, constraints and the precise claim with quantifiers.
State the task-to-model correspondence and its idealizations. Distinguish what is
observed or supplied from modeling choices being proposed now.

Use surveyed mathematical definitions and results where they fit. Link source
versions and inspected locations; map each needed assumption to the target.
If a required source is missing, request bounded research-survey work or proceed
with clearly labeled self-contained reasoning. Do not invent a theorem citation
to justify an otherwise valid elementary argument.

## Do useful mathematical work now

Within the reasoning allowance, attempt a useful lemma, bound, equivalence,
identifiability condition, counterexample or other precise consequence.
Show the actual argument, not just the name of a technique. Separate steps that
follow from definitions, imported results and unproved obligations. A proof sketch
is not a complete proof; a gap is an output when accompanied by its exact premises,
required conclusion, attempted approaches and the next discriminating step.

Check that the assumptions describe a nonempty, relevant problem rather than
assuming the desired conclusion. Examine boundary/degenerate cases, dimensions,
signs, quantifiers and necessary conditions as relevant. Distinguish statements
about every input, expectation, high probability and a particular finite sample.
Changing definitions or assumptions changes which claims the argument supports.

Use bounded symbolic/numerical calculations or explicit examples when they can
find an error or discriminate explanations. Record their actual execution and
scope. Finite successful examples do not prove a universal claim. An exact
counterexample satisfying all hypotheses refutes one; an approximate numerical
candidate needs precision and assumption checks before receiving that status.

For example, from a proposed finite update x_(t+1)=a*x_t+u_t, one can unroll the
recurrence and inspect how disturbance terms accumulate. This is work toward a
precise hypothesis; writing "study stability using control theory" is only a lead.
Whether the resulting bound is relevant or novel remains a separate question.

## Compare contributions, not mathematical decoration

Compare each candidate with the stable theme and cumulative pivot history from
[research-focus](../../research-focus/SKILL.md). Mathematical tools may change;
the important phenomenon and user boundaries remain the reference. A local lemma
needs a concrete claim link, not automatic promotion to the paper's thesis.

For each theoretical prototype, explain:

- What phenomenon, limitation or design choice the result clarifies.
- Which part is inherited, a direct application, a proposed extension or a new
  project result; how that distinction relates to closest work and search coverage.
- Whether the assumptions are plausible and the conclusion informative.
- What successful proof, counterexample or inconclusive work would change.
- Which prediction or assumption needs empirical checking, if any, and the
  cheapest useful check with resources and existing authorization.

Do not equate a new notation, renamed theorem or extra theory section with a new
contribution. An elementary application can still be useful explanatory support.
Preserve candidate uncertainty rather than promising novelty or acceptance.

Before topic selection, keep this effort bounded across credible candidates.
Recommend a direction using the actual mathematical progress, gaps and costs.
After selection, research-design deepens the proof and experimental protocol;
return here if the question itself must change. Preserve existing topic decisions.

## Deliver and iterate

Attach optional theory materials to the existing handoff: definition/assumption
revision, source-result links, statements and derivations, proof gaps or
counterexamples, actual check status and corresponding validation tasks.
Record author derivation, independent checking and machine verification distinctly;
none implies the others. A proof assistant is optional, not a new dependency.

For formal experiments in a theory-driven study, identify the problem,
assumptions, claim and proof status, prediction/validation target, approved protocol
and resources. A remaining proof gap does not bar already authorized informative
tests; label their conclusions as empirical. Mere experiments cannot complete that
gap. Pure-theory tasks may finish with a checked argument or a precise limitation.

Use existing round identifiers and decision records. Revise definitions explicitly
after counterexamples and identify affected proofs, evidence and paper claims.
Compare repairing the argument, narrowing the claim, changing the mathematical
tool, pursuing an empirical question, or closing the direction by expected learning
and cost. Continue within approval; changed core assumptions or expanded resources
use the existing affected review point. Do not iterate until a preferred result.
