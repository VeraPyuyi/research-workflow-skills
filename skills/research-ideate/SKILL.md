# Generate and Select Research Questions

Read the [shared contract](../research-navigate/references/workflow-contract.md)
and relevant [domain references](../research-navigate/references/domains/index.md).
Read [model routing](../research-navigate/references/model-routing.md) only when dispatching work to more than one model;
then use it to assign
native formalization/alternative branches and mathematical review consolidated at
M1 for full-paper work; bounded proof/counterexample work remains part of research.
Optional [evidence comparison and Jev](../research-navigate/references/decision-support.md) may assist a genuine
candidate choice; it is advisory and never decides a gate.
Read [reuse and simplification](../research-navigate/references/reuse-and-simplification.md)
before treating a mathematical prototype or candidate direction as new.

Use the brief, evidence map and existing decisions. If closest-work evidence is
missing, perform or request a bounded survey rather than asserting novelty.

## Start with a mathematical prototype

The brief's `theory_mode` decides this mode: `required` and `default` apply it,
`none` skips it for explicitly empirical or replication work. With `default`,
for open-ended new directions and method research, start theory first with
bounded exploratory checks. Read
[mathematical ideation](references/mathematical-ideation.md). For serious candidates,
define the objects, assumptions and target claim, connect relevant surveyed tools,
and attempt an actual derivation, lemma or counterexample before planning formal
experiments. Deliver the argument or exact gap; a future "develop theory" task
alone does not satisfy this mode.

Keep candidate work bounded before topic selection. Respect explicit empirical or
replication scope: define meaningful quantities without manufacturing a theorem.
Unavailable source details or project definitions remain gaps, not invented facts.
Pure theory need not acquire an experimental stage.

Prefer a sound prior theorem, standard construction or maintained implementation
over inventing equivalent notation. For each proposed reuse, check its conditions
and decide whether it supplies the result directly, needs an argument, requires a
changed model or is only an analogy. Reframe genuine new contribution around the
remaining mechanism, conditions, boundary or application.

## Compare genuine options

Use [research-focus](../research-focus/SKILL.md) to compare candidates against
the original theme and cumulative pivots. With in-theme delegation, select an
evidence-backed new question/method inside existing resources and record why its
value is preserved. A beautiful proof, easy benchmark or available dataset alone
does not justify downgrading the research question or changing the broad theme.

1. Express candidate contributions as questions or falsifiable hypotheses. Method,
   benchmark, replication, failure analysis and negative-result contributions are
   legitimate when they answer a meaningful question.
2. For each credible candidate, state why it matters, closest work and overlap,
   the proposed difference, observable supporting/refuting evidence, required
   data/tools, expected resources and principal risk.
   Include the strongest plausible objection, a simpler competing explanation
   or method, and the conditions under which the proposed advantage could fail.
   A complex method must earn its added complexity against a fairly tuned simple
   reference; do not add arbitrary quotas of ideas or experiments.
3. Identify the cheapest decisive check. Within the approved exploratory
   allowance, actually run it for the two leading candidates before asking for the
   topic decision, and report the outcome; a proposed check is not an executed one.
   Check the premise as well: confirm from sources or data that the phenomenon the
   candidate relies on is real before building on it.
4. Compare value, evidential strength, feasibility and cost. Remove exact duplicates
   of known contributions or infeasible candidates unless reframing produces a
   defensible question. Record which parts were simplified by reuse and what new
   contribution remains. State search coverage and remaining novelty uncertainty.
5. Generate candidates in at least two independent branches (different prompts,
   and different model lineages when available), then rank them by pairwise
   comparison in a context that did not generate them, recording each comparison
   and its reason. Ideas that read as novel often lose most value in execution, so
   weight the executed checks above the written pitch.
6. Recommend a direction with alternatives and consequences, then present the
   topic decision when it has not already been made.

## Deliver and finish

Deliver comparable topic proposals and a recommendation, with explicit assumptions
and a minimal validation plan. In theory-first mode, attach the versioned
definitions, actual theoretical prototype, source/assumption mapping, proof status
and discriminating predictions. Include the reuse decisions and the shared handoff.
When the user selects a topic, preserve that decision and route to research-design
to deepen the argument and design its validation within applicable authorization.
If later evidence defeats it, explain the change and offer redesign, a useful
negative finding, or stopping rather than repeatedly searching for a win.

Example: when a known method already uses the proposed memory mechanism, a
controlled study of failure recovery may be a different contribution; describe it
as a candidate question until the literature and topic decision support it.

## Mathematical work when applicable

Before adopting a decomposition, probe its hardest remainder within existing budget. Check whether the local theorem can apply to actual original inputs; independently existing witnesses do not imply a common witness.
Read [mathematical work](../research-navigate/references/mathematical-work.md) for the optional record and return contract.
