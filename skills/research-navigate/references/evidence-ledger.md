# Claim and Source Records

Use this reference when a survey, analysis or manuscript has substantive factual,
mathematical or empirical claims. Extend the project's existing records; a short
paper reading may use a compact table in its answer.

## Minimal claim record

| Field | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Claim identity/location/revision | Exact statement and manuscript, equation, table or code location |
| Kind and role | Source report, project observation, derivation, inference or hypothesis; contribution, support or background |
| Evidence and locator | Source/run identity, inspected version and exact section, page, equation, row or transformation |
| Access and coverage | Full text, selected passage, abstract, metadata or inaccessible; actual checks and exclusions |
| Conditions | Population/task, assumptions, quantifiers, metric, units, denominators and resource conditions |
| State | supported, limited, contradicted or unresolved; mathematical proof status stays separate |
| Gap and resolution | Why support is incomplete, smallest useful check, responsible stage and affected dependencies |

An unresolved record needs a concrete gap reason and next action, or an explicit
reason why the gap cannot be closed. Keep it visible until resolved, narrowed,
removed from the asserted claim set, or accepted as a limitation. Necessary
background does not need independent novelty; its factual claims still need
appropriate support.

## Two distinct citation checks

1. Resolve identity: title, authors, identifier, venue/version and duplicate
   reports of the same study. Metadata confirms identity, not the result.
2. Inspect support: read the passage needed for the attributed statement, including
   conditions, quantifiers, direction and uncertainty. A real paper can be cited
   for something it does not establish.

For important disputed claims, give a fresh reviewer answerable questions and
the primary material before revealing the author's preferred answer. Then compare
the independent extraction against the actual draft and record mismatches.
Do not withhold definitions or conditions that are necessary to answer correctly.
Neither a search snippet nor repeated summaries replace primary support.
Retrieval failure is an access gap, not proof of fabrication.

## Numerical and mathematical fidelity

Carry uncertainty type, level, sampling unit and calculation with each number.
Do not silently reinterpret `+/-`, standard deviation, standard error, range or a
confidence/credible interval as one another. If the type is missing, preserve the
reported value as untyped uncertainty and request its definition before drawing
an inferential conclusion. Matching aggregate values do not establish matching
denominators, units, definitions or inclusion rules.

Distinguish a false statement from an undefined expression or an example outside
the assumptions. Failure to satisfy a theorem's hypotheses does not establish
that its conclusion is false. An empirical exception to applicability and a
mathematical counterexample are different records.

## Change and resume

When a definition, metric, source version, inclusion rule or proof changes, mark
dependent claims, tables, code outputs and review decisions stale. Carry the old
record and reason for the change; do not reuse an old review badge for new content.
Restore the active round, checked artifact revisions, source coverage, open
findings, approvals, running IDs and resource commitments before new work.
This is an explicit handoff snapshot, not a claim that conversation memory persists.
