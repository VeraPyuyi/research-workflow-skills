# ICML Discovery Synthesis

Twelve discovery papers were read across 2024-2026: nine officially selected
papers and three ordinary accepted comparators. Each record identifies main-text
pages read, actual detail pages viewed, and uninspected appendices. These are
presentation observations, not certification of mathematical or empirical
claims. The parent completed the last paper from its full main text and rendered
pages after the first reader was interrupted; the first eleven were preserved.

Several papers organize technical material around reader questions.
Geometry-aware decoding moves from an objective to special regimes and
practical parameter choices (icml-2026-1, pp.2-5), while the gradient-consensus
control moves from diagnostic questions to an implementable mask
(icml-2026-5, pp.3-6). This transfers when the diagnostic distinguishes a
proposed component from alternatives. Naming a mechanism or organizing a smooth
narrative does not establish the cause of an improvement.

Causal papers clarify assumptions through concrete examples. Continuous-outcome
attribution starts from retrospective questions, specifies estimands and
assumptions, and returns to an example (icml-2025-1, pp.1-8).
Collaborative estimation separates sampling assumptions, a counterexample,
oracle estimators, nuisance estimation and application (icml-2024-5, pp.2-8).
Partial identification places interpretations beside formal results
(icml-2024-3, pp.4,6,8). The transferable feature is the correspondence, not
borrowing a diagram or relaxing conditions in an intuitive paraphrase.

Theory and practical algorithms often require different promises. Adaptive
experimental design distinguishes an ideal algorithm from a practical
approximation and records possible contradictory outputs
(icml-2024-1, pp.6-8). Causal velocity models repeat the score-estimation
condition in motivation, method and diagnostics
(icml-2025-5, pp.2,6,8-9). Exposition should expose which implementation inherits
which guarantee, rather than transfer an oracle result without an argument.

Useful displays make conditions visible at the comparison itself. Temperature
and model blocks in decoding tables (icml-2026-1, p.6), capability boundaries in
the watermark diagram (icml-2025-2, p.1), separate detection and steering panels
in AxBench (icml-2025-3, p.3), and aligned model-specification panels in
collaborative estimation (icml-2024-5, p.8) explain what is comparable.
Captions generally decode the graphic; the body connects it to the question.
Dense scatter labels, tiny formulas and unexplained emphasis sometimes weaken
that division of labor.

Counterexamples and mixed results remain important even with strong headlines.
The two-slice study reports dense-graph failures (icml-2024-2, p.7).
AxBench acknowledges concept-label dependence and complementary strengths
(icml-2025-3, p.9). Partial-identification simulations use a specific
uniform-simplex distribution (icml-2024-3, pp.7-8); this is not a universal
practical cutoff. The collaborative paper reports similar real-data effects
(icml-2024-5, p.8).

Selected and control papers both contain things not to imitate. Some text/table
comparisons disagree, boldface does not always identify the claimed winner, and
Figure 4 of the collaborative paper calls reference estimates true effects
despite a different description in the body. These conditional presentation
resources do not establish a cause of selection or acceptance probability.
